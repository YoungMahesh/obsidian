SSL stands for Secure Sockets Layer. 
It was developed by Netscape in 1990s to secure communication between web browsers and web servers.

versions: 
- 1994 - 1.0
- 1995 - 2.0
- 1996 - 3.0
- 1999 - TLS 1.0 : The IETF standardized a successor of SSL, based on SSL 3.0
- Today: Modern websites use TLS, not SSL. SSL versions are obsolete and insecure

---

Why do we still call it SSL?

The name survived in common usage.
Terms such as SSL certificate, SSL mode, SSL/TLS are still widely used, even though modern implementations use TLS.
